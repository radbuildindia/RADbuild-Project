Layout Approval

Site Layout Approval for Diagnostic Centres

A layout that looks workable on paper can still fail approval — or fail the equipment — if regulatory, structural and access requirements were never checked against each other. Layout approval is where those separate requirements have to agree.

What a layout has to satisfy at once

Room dimensions and clearances against the equipment minimum requirements. Shielding and access-control provisions. Structural adequacy for the loads involved. The delivery route the equipment will actually travel. Fire egress. Municipal building norms.

Each of these is straightforward on its own. The difficulty is that they are usually assessed by different people at different times, and they constrain each other. A layout signed off against each requirement individually can still be unbuildable.

Coordinating AERB, fire and municipal requirements

A layout satisfying AERB shielding and access rules can still conflict with fire egress requirements or municipal norms if those are addressed separately. The classic collision is a shielded wall placed where a required exit route has to run — resolvable at drawing stage, expensive once built.

We reconcile these into one buildable layout rather than leaving you to negotiate between conflicting sign-offs. See our AERB consultation page.

Layout mistakes that block approval later

  • Control rooms without adequate sightlines into the scan or procedure room
  • Insufficient clearance around the gantry for service access — legal, but it makes every service visit a problem
  • Shielding that conflicts with a required fire exit
  • Delivery routes never checked against the final room position
  • Plant and chiller locations with no serviceable access
  • Patient flow that routes through controlled areas

Every one of these is avoidable at layout stage and painful afterwards.

Drawings that the trades can actually build from

An approved layout is only useful if it survives contact with construction. We issue coordination drawings to every trade on site — civil, electrical, HVAC, shielding and interiors — so the approved layout is what gets built, rather than something approximating it.

Questions

Layout approval, answered.

Who approves a diagnostic centre layout?
Depending on modality and location it can involve AERB for radiation shielding and access control, municipal building authorities, and fire authorities — each with separate requirements the layout has to satisfy simultaneously.
Can a layout be approved after construction has started?
It is far riskier. Changes discovered late usually mean rework of finished construction. The layout should be finalised and checked against every applicable requirement before construction begins.
Our architect has already drawn a layout. Can you review it?
Yes, and we are often brought in exactly at that point. An architect who has not worked on imaging before will typically produce a layout that is sound as a building but misses equipment clearances, shielding continuity or the delivery route.
How does layout affect shielding cost?
Substantially. What occupies the spaces above, below and beside a scan room drives the shielding calculation, so moving a room within the same building can change the shielding cost materially.
